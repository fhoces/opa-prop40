Why do the two analyses of Proposition 40 disagree?

The BSZ analysis (Boll, Saez and Zucman, August 2026) finds that the billionaire tax raises money for California. The JR analysis (Jaros and Rauh, NBER, September 2026) finds that it loses money. This page puts both on one measure, the net fiscal effect: the wealth tax collected, plus extra income tax from asset sales, minus the income tax lost to people who leave.

Each slider moves one disputed input between the two analyses' values. Every dial has its smaller value at the left end and its larger value at the right end, and each end names whose value it is. But what are the actual quantities?

Each dial's two ends above give both analyses' values. The overview's table of differences sets them side by side with their sources.

Most of these are empirical quantities; H and r are scenario choices. Their values should not depend on whether one supports or opposes a wealth tax. An expert should be able to appraise each value from more to less credible.

Notes on the two estimates and the conventions

1 The BSZ number is this OPA's net total over 5 years at 3% (BSZ's own headline is the wealth tax alone), and the JR number is the authors' mean net present value in perpetuity. The paper prints −$38.9B, the average of one seeded run of 100,000 draws. The exact average of the same formula is −$38.98B, used throughout this page (more in the report: The two main estimates).

2 The equation adds up only when the terms are switched one after another, in this order, from left to right. In another order the same dials split the gap differently among the terms: moved first, q would take −$53.75B instead of −$39.44B, because it would also halve the tax on the billionaires who already left. The Apply buttons work from left to right, each unlocking the next, so each press changes the estimate by its amount in the equation (more in the report: The explorer's equation).

The net fiscal effect puts both on one definition, and the panels start at its two ends. The BSZ end is $106.8B and the JR end −$39.0B, a gap of $145.8B. The discount rate is a dial: 3% at the BSZ end, the rate the BSZ overview uses, and 2.73% at the JR end, the one rate worth the same as the authors' random draw of it (more in the report: The discount rate).

The litigation dial, q, is at 50% at the JR end. The JR explorer's litigation dial reads 1 at the main estimate, because there the revenue draw from 0 to the ceiling already halves the mean revenue. This explorer applies the same half as q = 50% of the ceiling (more in the report: The litigation dial).

Conventions. All amounts are billions of dollars. Present values are as of the date the wealth tax is assessed, and the first year of lost income tax is counted one year later (more in the report: Conventions).

Where the numbers come from: code, data and kinds

Where the numbers come from. Every number on this page is computed by comparison/R/ from the two analyses' export contracts (bsz-analysis/export/r/, rjkdc-analysis/export/r/: the files each pipeline writes for others to read) and the transcriptions in comparison/data/. The estimating function is score_common() in comparison/R/model.R, with a Python twin (R and Python agree to 1e-9 on every exported number; see comparison/tests). The browser runs model.js, a copy of it, and a test runs that copy with node over 600 slider mixes against the R values, to 1e-9. The report derives each number step by step and shows the bridge from the BSZ end to the JR end; the materials page lists the code, tests and files.

Data. ends.csv (the two ends, at each horizon setting), slider_sample.csv (300 slider mixes per panel with their values, the sample the tests run model.js on), inputs.csv (every disputed input and its sources), DISPUTES.md (the crosswalk of the disagreements, one row each, with what each analysis says and where), dial_path.csv (the terms of the equation at the top, dial by dial), comparison-data.js (what this page reads). A test fails if comparison-data.js or this page differs from the generator's output.

Kinds. Each end's tip ends with the kind of that analysis's value: data (settled by observable facts), research (a published estimate), guesswork (asserted without data or a study behind it), guesswork on data (a judgement applied to observed facts), scenario (a choice of which world is estimated) and derived (computed from the analysis's own inputs).

The function. The report states the estimating function line by line. The BSZ panel's sliders move only the BSZ bar and the JR panel's sliders only the JR bar.
