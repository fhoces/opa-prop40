TITLE: What are the actual quantities? Proposition 40, input by input

Proposition 40 would tax Californians worth $1 billion or more once, at 5% of their net worth. Estimates of what it is worth to the state depend on a handful of inputs. Each question shows one input and two values that have been proposed for it. Tell us which value you find more credible. Your answers will help us get closer to agreeing on common facts to inform important policy discussions.

Most of these are empirical quantities, and none should depend on whether one supports or opposes a wealth tax.

It takes 5 to 10 minutes. Answers are anonymous unless you leave an email near the end.

For each input, pick the value you find more credible.

--- 1. The income tax lost when billionaires leave ---

Q3. The share of billionaires' income tax lost when people leave: which is more credible? (required)
   (1) A. 45% of all the income tax that billionaires pay: more than the 29% of their wealth reported to have left, because income tax need not leave in step with wealth and some moves go unreported.
   (2) B. 5% of all the income tax that billionaires pay: the same as the 5% of their wealth assumed to leave, because the largest of the billionaires reported to have left pay little income tax for their wealth.
   (3) About equally credible
   (4) Unsure

   (1) A. For ever: billionaires who leave are assumed not to return.
   (2) B. 5 years, about the payment period, treating the departures as temporary.
   (3) About equally credible
   (4) Unsure

Q7. Billionaires' yearly California income tax: which is more credible? (required)
   (1) A. $4.55 billion a year: from California tax data, taking the billionaires to be among the state's 212 to 500 highest earners.
   (2) B. $3.06 billion a year: from California tax data, taking the billionaires to have about half the income of the state's highest earners, as a study of top wealth holders finds.
   (3) About equally credible

--- 2. Discounting the revenue for court risk ---

Q9. Discounting the revenue for the chance that courts strike the tax down: which is more credible? (required)

   (2) B. A discount: the wealth tax collected is multiplied by the chance the tax survives in court, put at 50%.
   (3) About equally credible

--- 3. Billionaires who moved out in time ---

Q11. Billionaires who moved out of California before the 1 January 2026 residency date: which is more credible? (required)
   (1) A. About 29% of the wealth: seven billionaires (Page, Thiel, Hankey, Kalanick, Brin, Spielberg and Sacks) are counted as having moved in time, on public reports of their moves.
   (2) B. 5% of the wealth: an allowance for billionaires who moved in time, with no one counted by name without evidence of a change of legal residence before the date.
   (3) About equally credible

Q14. Wealth that escapes the tax through avoidance and evasion, beyond moving away: which is more credible? (optional)

Q15. The billionaires' wealth the tax applies to: which is more credible? (optional)
   (1) A. The Forbes list of 1 July 2026: 250 California billionaires worth $2,307 billion, or $2,157 billion without the non-US citizens asked about next.
   (2) B. The Forbes list of 2025: 212 California billionaires, grown 7% to the 31 December 2026 valuation date: $2,027 billion.
   (3) About equally credible

Q16. Non-US-citizen residents: which is more credible? (optional)

   (3) About equally credible
   (4) Unsure

Q17. Real-estate deduction: which is more credible? (optional)
