# PAP review, 2026-10-18: registry standards

This review checks `comparison/survey/PRE-ANALYSIS-PLAN.md` (the plan), `comparison/survey/power/power.tex` with `sim.py` (the appendix), the two form copies and `comparison/survey/registry-draft.md` against what the AEA RCT Registry and a demanding PAP referee expect (the registry's fields; McKenzie's checklist; Olken 2015; Duflo et al. 2020; J-PAL and BITSS guidance). The egress test failed: `curl -sS -m 20 -o /dev/null -w '%{http_code}\n' https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/` returned `000` with "CONNECT tunnel failed, response 403", so the registry's current field list was not consulted and the review works from memory of it. Line numbers refer to the plan unless another file is named, quoted current text is given with the plan's line wraps removed, and the findings are ordered most important first.

1. **Section 5.3 and 6.1, lines 241 and 266-267. Severity A. AUTHOR DECISION** (touches the 2026-10-18 decision that per-question multinomial outcomes are dropped).
   - What is wrong: S3 is defined as "Each of the five $y_{ik}$, reported as three indicators (BSZ value, JR value, neutral answer)", and section 6.1 says "The five S3 outcomes are adjusted with the Romano-Wolf step-down procedure within each answer type". Two analysts would run different analyses: (a) five regressions with $y_{ik}$ (coded +1, -1, 0) as the outcome, one Romano-Wolf family of five, which is what the appendix's tier 1 simulates (`sim.py` line 284, `Y = y`; `thresholds.tex` row 1, "Tests m = 5") and what the pretest analysis ran (`comparison/survey/pilot_analysis.py` lines 140-152; `feedback/009-2026-10-17-rct-pilot-2/analysis/s3.csv`); or (b) fifteen regressions, one per indicator and question, in three families of five ("within each answer type"), for which the appendix gives no threshold. The registered text must say which.
   - Proposed fix (reading (a), the one the appendix and the code support). Line 241, replace "- S3. Each of the five $y_{ik}$, reported as three indicators (BSZ value, JR value, neutral answer)." with "- S3. Each of the five $y_{ik}$, coded as in section 5.1, as the outcome of the H1 equation. For each question, the shares of the three answers (the BSZ value, the JR value, a neutral answer) are also reported by arm, descriptively." Lines 266-267, replace "Secondary outcomes S1 to S3 are estimated with the same equation. The five S3 outcomes are adjusted with the Romano-Wolf step-down procedure within each answer type (section 6.3)." with "Secondary outcomes S1 to S3 are estimated with the same equation; for S3 the outcome is $y_{ik}$. The five S3 tests form one family, adjusted with the Romano-Wolf step-down procedure (section 6.3)." In `registry-draft.md` lines 49-50, replace "S3 each of the five questions separately." with "S3 each of the five questions separately, coded +1, -1 and 0 as the index's inputs." If the author intends reading (b), tier 1 of the appendix must be re-simulated with fifteen tests in three families before registration.

2. **Section 7, lines 380-384. Severity A.**
   - What is wrong: "if the appendix is regenerated, it governs" lets the thresholds that decide which analyses run change after registration without a logged deviation. The plan also says the rule is applied "at the realized shares" (lines 373 and 380) but not how a simulated Romano-Wolf threshold is recomputed at realized shares, when, or by whom. The appendix says "At the close all rows are recomputed at the realized shares" (`power.tex` line 419), which `sim.py` cannot do without editing its constants `P_EXPL` and `SHARES0` (lines 47-48).
   - Proposed fix. Replace lines 380-384, from "The rule uses only N" to "so its thresholds lean high.", with: "The rule uses only N and the realized shares of pre-treatment subgroups at the close, so it does not depend on the results. At the close, before any outcome is tabulated, `sim.py` is rerun once with the realized shares in place of the assumed ones (60/40 for the entry point, 50/50 for expertise and prior exposure, a quarter for the H5 subgroup), and the regenerated `out/thresholds.tex` is committed together with the realized shares. Those thresholds decide which analyses run. An analysis below its threshold is reported as "not run: below its threshold", with the threshold. Until then the registered appendix (`out/summary.tex` and `out/thresholds.tex`) governs. No other regeneration is foreseen; any other change to the appendix after registration is a deviation and is logged (section 9). The appendix's simulations use 199 bootstrap draws inside each simulated sample, fewer than the registered 2,000, so its thresholds lean high."

3. **Section 6.1, lines 263-264, and section 9, lines 433-435. Severity B.**
   - What is wrong: "The robustness specification adds the two pre-treatment answers as indicators" is ambiguous. Each question has three answers, so "as indicators" can mean two dummies per question or the binary codings of H3 and H4. Section 9 adds a third covariate to "the robustness specification of H1" (the after-the-vote indicator) that section 6.1 does not mention, and does not say what happens when no response comes after the vote.
   - Proposed fix. Replace "The robustness specification adds the two pre-treatment answers as indicators." with "The robustness specification adds to the H1 equation indicator variables for the two pre-treatment answers (for expertise, "Yes" and "Somewhat", with "No" as the base; for prior exposure, "Yes" and "Not sure", with "No" as the base) and the after-the-vote indicator of section 9, which is dropped if every response was submitted before the vote. It is reported alongside the main estimate and does not replace it." (If the author prefers the binary codings of section 6.2, write "the subgroup indicators G of H3 and H4" in place of the two dummies per question; either reading is acceptable, but one must be registered.)

4. **Section 6.2, lines 305-310, and section 7, line 369. Severity B.**

   - Proposed fix. After line 310 ("... 12 for two (3 pairs x 4 cells), and 8 for three.") add: "The effect inside a cell is the coefficient on T in the H1 equation estimated on that cell's responses only, with HC2 standard errors. Tier 5 of section 7 applies the same estimator to each $y_{ik}$ inside each half of each dimension: 5 questions x 3 dimensions x 2 halves, 30 tests in one Romano-Wolf family."

8. **Section 7, lines 331-334 and 361. Severity B. AUTHOR DECISION** (adds a rule for very small samples).
   - What is wrong: H1 is "tested whatever the number of responses", but the appendix verified the test's level only from N = 30 (`power.tex` lines 333-335), and the plan says nothing about a stratum with responses in one arm only, which contributes nothing to β. With an expected range of 0 to 200 responses, N below 30 is likely.
   - Proposed fix. After line 334 ("... with 80% power.") add: "H1 is estimated whenever at least one entry-point stratum has responses in both arms. A stratum with responses in one arm only is dropped from the H1 equation, and the count dropped is reported. Below 30 responses, the smallest N at which the appendix verified the test's level, the p-value of H1 comes from randomization inference, re-drawing T within each stratum 10,000 times, in place of the HC2 t-test; the estimate and its HC2 confidence interval are reported as well."

9. **Section 8, lines 399-409. Severity B. AUTHOR DECISION** (adds an analysis).
   - What is wrong: the attrition checks are "reported whatever the result", but the plan does not say what is done when they show differential attrition, which is the one way the comparison page can bias H1 (line 399-400). Standard practice is to pre-specify bounds.
   - Proposed fix. After item 3 (line 409) add: "4. H1 is also reported with Lee (2009) trimming bounds, whatever the gap in submission rates: within each entry point, the arm with the higher rate (submissions divided by draws) is trimmed of the share of its responses that equalizes the two rates, once from the top and once from the bottom of the lean index, and the two estimates bracket the effect. With equal rates the bounds coincide with the estimate. If the draw count fails, the bounds are not computed and the binomial check stands alone."
