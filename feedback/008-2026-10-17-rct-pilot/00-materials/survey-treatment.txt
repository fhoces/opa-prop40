TITLE: What are the actual quantities? Proposition 40, input by input

Proposition 40 would tax Californians worth $1 billion or more once, at 5% of their net worth. Estimates of what it is worth to the state depend on a handful of inputs. Each question shows one input, two values that have been proposed for it, and the reason given for each. Tell us which value you find more credible.

These are empirical quantities: their values should not depend on whether one supports or opposes a wealth tax.

It takes about 5 minutes. Answers are anonymous unless you leave an email near the end.

=== Page 2: How the two estimates differ ===
Two academic studies estimate what Proposition 40 is worth to California:
• Boll, Saez and Zucman (BSZ, August 2026): +$106.8 billion.
• Jaros and Rauh (JR, NBER, September 2026): −$39.0 billion.

The gap is $145.8 billion. Both are on one measure: the wealth tax collected, plus the extra income tax from assets sold to pay it, minus the income tax lost when people leave.

Two parts explain 83% of the gap: the income tax lost when billionaires leave, and the chance the tax survives in court. Below, how much of the gap each part explains, and the values each study uses.

1. The income tax lost when billionaires leave: the share lost, how long it is counted, and billionaires' yearly income tax, together
████████████████████ $81.3 billion, 56% of the gap
BSZ: only the leavers' own income tax is lost; counted for 5 years; $3.06 billion a year.
JR: 45% of all billionaires' income tax is lost; counted for ever; $4.55 billion a year.

2. The chance the tax survives a court challenge
██████████ $39.4 billion, 27% of the gap
BSZ: 100%.
JR: 50%.

3. Billionaires counted as having already left
███████ $29.9 billion, 21% of the gap
BSZ: no named billionaire (its allowance for 5% of the wealth moving away in time is in part 4).
JR: seven publicly reported moves.

4. Other inputs together: the wealth on the list, avoidance, and three smaller inputs
░ +$4.9 billion, offsetting 3% of the gap

The four amounts are the four terms of the equation on the project's comparison page, with the inputs moved one after another from the BSZ values to the JR values. The income tax lost is a product of the share lost, how long it is counted and the yearly amount, so it is shown as one amount: splitting it among those three would depend on the order in which they are changed. The other amounts also change somewhat with the order.

The questions that follow ask which study's value of each input you find more credible.

Q3. The share of billionaires' income tax lost when people leave: which is more credible? (required)

Q7. Billionaires' yearly California income tax: which is more credible? (required)
   (1) JR: $4.55 billion a year: the middle of a range from California tax data. The top, $5.8 billion, assumes the billionaires are the state's 212 highest earners. The bottom, $3.3 billion, assumes they are spread among the top 500 tax filers.
   (2) BSZ: $3.06 billion a year: the income tax paid by California's highest earners, halved, because research finds the wealthiest people have about half the income of the highest earners.
   (3) About equally credible
   (4) Unsure

--- 2. The chance the tax survives in court ---

Q9. The chance the tax survives a court challenge: which is more credible? (required)
   (1) BSZ: 100%: the tax is collected in full, as written.
   (2) JR: 50%: a guesstimated likelihood that the tax will not survive court litigation; it is as likely to fail as to stand.
   (3) About equally credible
   (4) Unsure

--- 3. Billionaires who moved out in time ---

Q11. Billionaires who moved out of California before the 1 January 2026 residency date: which is more credible? (required)
   (1) JR: About 29% of the wealth: seven billionaires with publicly reported moves (Page, Thiel, Hankey, Kalanick, Brin, Spielberg and Sacks) are counted as having moved in time.
   (2) BSZ: 5% of the wealth: an allowance for billionaires who managed to move in time. No named billionaire is counted, because press reports do not by themselves establish a change of legal residence. Counting four of them (Page, Thiel, Hankey and Kalanick) is treated as an aggressive scenario.
   (3) About equally credible
   (4) Unsure

Q14. Wealth that escapes the tax through avoidance and evasion, beyond moving away: which is more credible? (optional)

Q15. The billionaires' wealth the tax applies to: which is more credible? (optional)
   (1) BSZ: The Forbes list of 1 July 2026: 250 California billionaires worth $2,307 billion.
   (2) JR: The Forbes list of 2025: 212 California billionaires, grown 7% to the 31 December 2026 valuation date: $2,027 billion.
   (3) About equally credible
   (4) Unsure

Q16. Non-US-citizen residents: which is more credible? (optional)

   (3) About equally credible
   (4) Unsure

Q17. Real-estate deduction: which is more credible? (optional)

   (4) Unsure
