# Why do the two analyses of Proposition 40 disagree?

The [BSZ analysis] (Boll, Saez and Zucman, August
2026) finds that the billionaire tax raises money for California. The
[JR analysis] (Jaros and Rauh, NBER, September
2026) finds that it loses money. This page puts both on one measure, the net fiscal
effect: the wealth tax collected, plus extra income tax from asset sales, minus the income
tax lost to people who leave.

(image: )

BSZ main estimate^[1]
[$106.8B]
Over 5 years: the wealth tax, net of the income tax it adds and loses.

JR main estimate^[1]
[−$39.0B]
Net present value: the wealth tax minus the income tax lost for ever, in today's dollars.

## What separates the two numbers

$106.80B BSZ estimate
− $29.89B Billionaires already departed (D)
− $39.44B Chance the tax does not survive litigation (q)
− $81.34B Lost income tax (H, f, C, r)
+ $4.89B The other three steps together (W, a, the minor switch)
= −$38.98B JR estimate

Each amount is what its dials change when they are moved to the JR values, term by term from left to right, as the Walk the path button does.^[2]

## The inputs

Each slider moves one disputed input between the two analyses' values.
Every dial has its smaller value at the left end and its larger value at the right end, and
each end names whose value it is. The BSZ panel starts at the BSZ values, the JR panel at the
JR values, so a dial can start at either end. Tap or hover over a symbol or an end for what it
means and the [kind] of each value.

### But what are the actual quantities?

Each dial's two ends above give both analyses' values. The
[overview's table of differences] sets them side by
side with their sources.

Most of these are empirical quantities; H and r are scenario choices. Their values should not depend on whether one
supports or opposes a wealth tax. An expert should be able to appraise each value from more to less
credible.

Notes on the two estimates and the conventions

^1 Each analysis can be run at many settings. The BSZ overview leads with $106.8B, this OPA's net total. The JR overview leads with the printed −$38.9B. This page uses the exact average of the same formula, −$38.98B, shown as −$39.0B. Each is defined its own way. The BSZ
number is a net total over 5 years: the wealth tax (Table 5 row 1), plus extra income
tax from assets sold to pay it, minus the income tax lost to billionaires who leave, for 5
years at 3%. BSZ's own headline is the wealth tax alone; the net total is this OPA's choice,
explained on the [BSZ analysis overview]. The JR
number is a perpetuity net present value, averaged over the authors' Monte Carlo draws (a
simulation that draws the uncertain inputs at random many times and averages the results)
(Jaros and Rauh, NBER chapter c15504, Tax Policy and the Economy vol. 41, September 2026).
The paper prints −$38.9B, the average of one seeded run of 100,000 draws. The exact average of the same formula is −$38.98B, used throughout this page ([report], [Step 6 of the JR analysis's report]). A seeded run starts the random draws from a fixed number, so it gives the same draws every time.

^2 The equation adds up only when the terms are switched one after another, in this order, from left to right. In another order the same dials split the gap differently among the terms: moved first, q would take −$53.75B instead of −$39.44B , because it would also halve the tax on the billionaires who already left. Move the BSZ panel's dials to their far ends, term by term from left to right. The D and q terms are those two steps of that walk. Lost income tax is the four loss-box dials of the walk added up (H, f, C and r). The last term is the other three steps added up (W, a and the minor switch). H is listed first because its step is the largest. The walk moves the four in the order f, C, H and r. The Walk the path button in each panel does it for you. The JR panel's button walks it backwards, from the last term to the first. The [report] gives all nine steps. Each amount is what its group changes when the dials move in this order. It is not a total, and its size depends on the order, because the inputs multiply each other: a departure matters more when more wealth is left to tax. The report's [order-free check] averages each input's step over every order the dials could move in. The amounts are rounded to two decimals and add up exactly to the JR estimate, −$38.98B . For scale, the lost income tax itself is worth $75.0B today in the JR analysis and $0.7B in the BSZ analysis. The Apply buttons work from left to right, each unlocking the next, so each press changes the estimate by its amount in the equation.

The net fiscal effect puts both on one definition, and the panels start at its
two ends. The BSZ end is $106.8B and the JR end −$39.0B , a gap of $145.8B .
The discount rate is a dial: 3% at the BSZ end, the rate the BSZ overview uses, and
2.73% at the JR end, the one rate worth the same as the authors' random draw of it.

The litigation dial, q, is at 50% at the JR end. The JR explorer's litigation
dial reads 1 at the main estimate, because there the revenue draw from 0 to the ceiling
already halves the mean revenue. This explorer applies the same half as q = 50% of the
ceiling.

Conventions. All amounts are billions of dollars. Present values are as of the
date the wealth tax is assessed, and the first year of lost income tax is counted one year
later. The [report's conventions] cover the face-value
wealth tax, the Act's installments and deferral account, and the JR analysis's r − g rate.

Where the numbers come from: code, data and kinds

Where the numbers come from. Every number on this page is computed by
comparison/R/ from the two analyses' export contracts
( bsz-analysis/export/r/ , rjkdc-analysis/export/r/ : the files each
pipeline writes for others to read) and the transcriptions in comparison/data/ .
The estimating function is score_common() in comparison/R/model.R ,
with a Python twin (R and Python agree to 1e-9 on every exported number; see comparison/tests). The browser runs [ model.js ],
a copy of it, and a test runs that copy with node over 600 slider mixes
against the R values, to 1e-9. The [report] derives each number
step by step and shows the bridge from the BSZ end to the JR end; the
[materials page] lists the code, tests and files.

Data. [ends.csv] (the two ends, at
each horizon setting),
[slider_sample.csv] (300 slider mixes per
panel with their values, the sample the tests run model.js on),
[inputs.csv] (every disputed input and its sources),
[DISPUTES.md]
(the crosswalk of the disagreements, one row each, with what each analysis says and where),
[dial_path.csv] (the terms of the equation at the top, dial by dial),
[comparison-data.js] (what this page reads).
A test fails if comparison-data.js or this page differs from the generator's
output.

Kinds. Each end's tip ends with the kind of that analysis's value:
data (settled by observable facts), research (a published estimate),
guesswork (asserted without data or a study behind it), guesswork on data (a
judgement applied to observed facts), scenario (a choice of which world is
estimated) and derived (computed from the analysis's own inputs).

The function. The [report] states
the estimating function line by line. The BSZ panel's sliders move only the BSZ bar and the JR
panel's sliders only the JR bar.
