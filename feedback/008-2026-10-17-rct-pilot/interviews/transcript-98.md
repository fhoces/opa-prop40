# Interview transcript: respondent 98 (share, control)

## Round 1 (questions 1 to 7)

1. I think the survey was meant to get people's view on which number for each part of the tax seemed more believable. The entry note said it was for experts on the points where two analyses disagree. I am not an expert, so I was unsure why I was in it, but I went on.

2. The note said it would take about five minutes, that it named neither analysis, and that the authors wanted expert views. I expected a short set of questions for specialists. The survey ran about fourteen minutes and asked for reasons under most questions. It did not name the studies, though I had read about these estimates before, so some figures looked familiar without my knowing where they came from.

3. It felt long, about fourteen minutes. Q7 took the longest, because I read the three billionaire figures and the groups behind them more than once. The five optional questions at the end went quickly once I decided to answer them.

4. I did not skip any required question. I left the "why" boxes blank after Q5, Q9 and Q18 (the Q19 box), because I had no basis to give a reason. I did not answer the optional email step (Q21), because I did not want to leave an email.

5. I would make "Unsure" and "About equally credible" easier to tell apart, since I used "Unsure" for things I did not know and never needed "About equally". I would drop "guesstimated" from Q9, because that word made the 50% option sound like a guess, and I did not know how to weigh a guess against the 100% option. I would also tell people on the first page that it takes longer than five minutes, since it took me about fourteen.

6. The A or B letter was just a tag for each option, and I did not read it as naming who proposed the value. The entry note said two analyses disagree, so I assumed each pair of values came from them. I could not tell which analysis each value came from, and the survey did not say, so I cannot say which side a value belonged to.

7. "About equally credible" meant that neither value seemed more believable than the other. I never chose it, because each question had one side that looked better to me, even if I was not sure. "Unsure" meant I did not know enough to judge. I chose it for Q5 (how long the lost tax counts), Q9 (whether the tax survives court), Q17 (the real-estate deduction) and Q18 (tax on assets sold to pay the tax). I left the reasons blank for Q5, Q9 and the Q19 box after Q18, and Q17 had no reason box.

## Round 2 (questions 8 to 12)

8. Not quite. Q9 B says "guesstimated likelihood" and "as likely to fail as to stand", which sounds like a made-up number, while A reads as a plain fact ("collected in full, as written"). Q11 B spends most of its words on why no named billionaire counts, and calls the four-name case "aggressive", while A just lists the seven names. Q3 B's "only the income tax of those who leave" uses "only", which made it sound like the narrower and more sensible choice to me. Q5 B's "treating the responses as temporary" was unclear, so I could not weigh it against A.

9. Q3 and Q9 are answers to the same question, so I could weigh them: one is a share of tax lost, the other a chance of surviving court. For Q3 the difference is scope, since A counts everyone's income tax and B counts only the leavers' tax. For Q9 the difference is basis, because A is a flat assumption of full collection and B is described as a guess with no source. Q11 is also the same question, a share of wealth that moved in time, but B's 5% is an allowance and A's 29% comes from a named list. I am not sure whether the list and the allowance can be weighed against each other, since they are built in different ways.

10. The Q3 choice that fits is B, "5%, only the income tax of those who leave". The 29% in Q11 says how much wealth moved in time, and it does not change the rule for what is lost, so a person who believes both would take B. The 5% in B is a share of the income tax, though, not the share of people who leave, so I would be careful not to read it as the same 5% from Q11 B. I was not sure about this distinction when I read the survey.

## Follow-up

1. A says the state loses 45% of all billionaires' income tax, and it says that figure is set above the share who leave, to cover moves nobody reports. To me that means everyone's tax is counted, so I read it as a cushion, not as a measure. B says the state loses only the income tax of people who leave, in step with the share of wealth that leaves, so 5% of wealth gives 5% and 29% gives 29%. I would pick B now, the same as in the original Q3, and the new wording makes that choice easier because it spells out the proportion.

2. Q9 new B reads better to me now. "The threshold an accounting rule uses" sounds like a standard, while A says the Act was "argued to have been drafted" to avoid challenges, which is a hedge, so A seems less sure of itself. In Q11 new, B gives a reason for not counting the named people ("a press report of a move is not a record of a change of legal residence"), while A only lists seven names with nothing about how they were checked. So B is the fuller answer in Q11, though I still find A's names easier to check.

3. B is shorter and gives less of a reason. It says "from tax data" and "halved" and then "research finds the wealthiest people have about half the income", which is a claim I cannot check from the text. A says where the range comes from, the two groups it assumes, and why the number is in the middle. I would say A is described more fully, and that is the one I would trust more.
