## Their out-of-character notes (not shown to the interviewer)
Respondent 56 read the entry note as a call for expert views on a tax study, and took the survey to be a neutral check on the inputs behind the two estimates. The page-2 table and the phrase "BSZ" and "JR" were not known to them; they read BSZ and JR as the names of the two studies, and A and B are not in this survey (the labels are the study names). The entry note set the expectation that the survey was about inputs, not about whether the tax is good, so they answered each input on its own and did not reward either side. They found the four-part split on page 2 hard to compare with the input questions because the split amounts do not map one-to-one to the questions. They marked Unsure on the technical inputs (share lost, yearly income tax, avoidance, base, citizens, real estate, asset sales) because they could not judge them, and leaned on their general sense of courts and of who leaves on the court and residency questions.
