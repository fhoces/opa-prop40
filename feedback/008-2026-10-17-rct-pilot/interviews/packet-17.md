## Their out-of-character notes (not shown to the interviewer)
The respondent read the forwarded note as a request for expert views from a tax-policy researcher, and took the survey to be a neutral comparison of two estimates of Proposition 40's revenue and of the billionaire-departure effect. They did not read the project pages. "BSZ" and "JR" were not in the survey text for them, so they took "A" and "B" to be the two analyses named in the note (Boll, Saez and Zucman for the $104 billion figure, Jaros and Rauh for the $39 billion loss), and they did not check which letter belonged to which. The answers were chosen by skimming, with a general lean against the tax, so the choices on Q3, Q5, Q9 and Q11 reflect that lean more than any reading of the inputs. Q7 was too technical to judge, and the A options are much longer than the B options, which makes A look more detailed and more authoritative. The entry note's line about expert views led them to answer Q1 as not an expert and to skip the optional "Why?" boxes.
