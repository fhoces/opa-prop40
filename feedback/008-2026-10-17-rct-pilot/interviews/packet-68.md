## Their out-of-character notes (not shown to the interviewer)
The persona read the explorer page's top half and the survey intro, then skimmed the questions. They took the survey to be a poll on which of two sets of assumptions behind the two Prop 40 estimates looked more credible, not a test of the estimates themselves. "A" and "B" were read as the two sides' positions, with A (45%, 5-year-or-forever, $4.55B) seen as the larger-loss side, which the persona leaned toward given their view of wealth taxes. They skipped the long "Why?" explanations and the optional five-input block, so the survey's claim that these are empirical quantities did not register much. The entry text's "BSZ" and "JR" labels were not clear from the survey itself, which uses only A and B and never names the analyses, so the persona had to infer which side was which. The entry message ("what do you think these values are?") set an expectation of a quick expert check, and the persona answered it as a rough lean rather than a considered judgement.
