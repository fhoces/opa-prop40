## Round 2 (questions 8 to 12)

8. Yes, mostly, but not on every question. In Q7 the JR value gets a long explanation (the 212 highest earners, the top 500 filers, the range from $3.3 to $5.8 billion), while the BSZ value is one sentence about halving the top earners' tax. In Q9 the JR value calls its 50% "a guesstimated likelihood," and that word made it sound weaker to me than the BSZ "collected in full, as written." 9. Q3 and Q9 were two answers to the same question, so I could weigh one against the other. In Q3 both values are a share of the same tax lost, one at 45% of all billionaires and one at 5% of leavers. In Q9 both are a chance that the tax survives, 100% and 50%. Q11 was different: the JR value names seven people and says they are counted as having moved, while the BSZ value is a general 5% allowance that names no one. So the two Q11 values answer slightly different questions, and I could not weigh them directly.

10. The Q3 option that fits that view is BSZ's: "5%, only the income tax of those who leave." If I believed the 29% of wealth had moved in time, I would still take only the leavers' own tax as the loss, because 5% of billionaires' tax is then what leaves. I am not sure that combination is consistent in every detail, because a 29% move would make the lost share depend on who is counted as gone. I did not work that out when I answered.

11. That page told me the two estimates differ by about $146 billion and that the two biggest reasons are the income tax lost and the court chance. 12. The page changed my attention more than my answers. Because it said the income tax lost was the biggest part, I gave Q3, Q5 and Q7 more thought, and I still answered "Unsure" on Q3 and Q7 because I could not judge them. The BSZ and JR labels did not change any answer I can point to, since I was already unsure which side each one was on. For Q9 I chose JR's 50% because I think a new tax like this will be sued, which is my own reason and not something the page said.

## Follow-up

1. The JR choice says a flat 45% of all billionaires' income tax is lost, no matter how many of them leave, so even the ones who stay count as lost. The BSZ choice says only the tax of the people who leave is lost, in step with how much wealth leaves, so 5% of wealth leaving means 5% of the tax lost. I would now pick BSZ, because the people who stay are still paying the tax, so counting their tax as lost seems wrong to me. That is different from my original Q3 answer, which was "Unsure," because the new BSZ wording tells me what the number is tied to.

2. Yes, the BSZ value in Q9 is described in a better light. It says the Act was "drafted to stay clear of constitutional challenges," which sounds like the authors took care over it, while the JR value says "as likely to stand as to fail" and then explains a threshold from an accounting rule, which reads as technical and less certain. In Q11 the JR value still names the seven people, which makes it concrete, while the BSZ value now says a press report "is not a record" of a legal move, which sounds like a good reason to me. I would say the two Q11 values are now about even, and in Q9 the BSZ wording reads better to me.

3. Yes, the JR value in Q7 is described more fully. It gives the range from $3.3 billion to $5.8 billion and names the two ways of spreading the billionaires across the earners, which is more detail than the BSZ value, which only says "halved." The BSZ value does give a reason, that the wealthiest people have about half the income of the top earners, but the JR value's range makes it look more careful to me.
