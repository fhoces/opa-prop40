## Their out-of-character notes (not shown to the interviewer)
Respondent 98 read the entry note as a call for expert views on a tax-policy question, and the "not an expert" answer at Q1 did not stop them from going on. They took the survey to be a test of which of two estimates is more believable, and they read the A and B labels as the two studies (A as the Boll, Saez and Zucman estimate, B as Jaros and Rauh) even though the survey says it names neither. The entry note did not explain what the two numbers in it mean, so they relied on the question text. Comparing the two values on each input was hard because the A and B options differ in several ways at once (a range against a single halved figure, a named list against an allowance), and the respondent said so in the free-text reasons. The Unsure choices at Q5, Q9, Q17 and Q18 reflect a lack of ability to judge, not indifference. Answers are simulated for a pilot and do not come from a real person.
