# Interview transcript: respondent 85 (share, treatment)

## Round 1 (questions 1 to 7)

1. It was a survey asking which value of each input you find more credible for Proposition 40. The page said the answers were empirical and should not depend on whether you support the tax. I took it as a check on the numbers behind the two estimates.

2. The note from my colleague said the survey "names neither analysis," and it said it would take about five minutes. I expected a neutral list of inputs. Page 2 then named Boll, Saez and Zucman and Jaros and Rauh and gave their totals, so the survey was less blind than the note said. The five minutes was about right, and I finished in about four.

3. The survey felt short and moved quickly. The longest part was the value text on Q7 and Q11, because those options carry several numbers and conditions. Q13 also took a moment, since I had to decide whether the smaller inputs were worth it.

4. I skipped Q14 to Q18 on purpose. Page 4 said these inputs have only a small effect, and I had already said no to assessing them on Q13. I left Q6, Q8, Q19 and Q20 blank because the value text and my Q4 and Q10 answers already said what I thought, and I could not think of a missing input.

5. I would name the two sources on the first page, not only on page 2, so the first page matches the note. I would also tell you before the first question that the list has an optional "why" box for each input, because I skipped several without knowing whether they mattered. I would not change the values themselves, though I think the long Q7 and Q11 wording is heavy for a quick survey.

6. The "JR:" and "BSZ:" labels meant the team whose estimate the value came from, and I read them as the authors' names. The idea came from page 2, which named both studies, and from my own reading of the literature. I knew Rauh's migration work, which is why Q3 pointed me toward the 45% value. I did not treat the label as a verdict on either study.

7. I did not choose "About equally credible" or "Unsure" on any question, so I can only say what I took them to mean. "About equally credible" would mean both values are defensible to me, and "Unsure" would mean I could not judge which was better. I did not need either, because on each of the required questions I had an opinion, even if it was a weak one.

## Round 2 (questions 8 to 12)

8. Not quite. The JR text in Q7 is much longer, with the three ranges and the 212 and 500 filer counts, while the BSZ text is one line of reasoning. In Q9 the JR value calls itself a "guesstimated likelihood," and that word made me read JR's 50% as a guess and BSZ's 100% as the record. In Q11 BSZ's text says its own number is "treated as an aggressive scenario" for the four-name case, which sounds like a judgment against that case, while JR's text simply lists seven names.

9. In Q3 both values are a share of the same income tax, so I could weigh one against the other. In Q9 both are a probability of surviving court, so they are comparable too. In Q11 both are a share of the wealth that moved out in time, so they are also comparable, but the reasons differ: BSZ's text gives a rule about domicile, while JR's gives a list of named people. The Q9 and Q11 pairs are the same kind of number, while the reasons behind them are not.

10. I am not sure any choice in Q3 fits that view exactly. If 29% of the wealth left and only the leavers' income tax is lost, the share of billionaires' income tax lost should be roughly 29%, which is neither 5% nor 45%. The BSZ option says "only the income tax of those who leave," so it is the closer wording, but its 5% assumes far fewer people leave than 29%. I would have wanted an option at around 29%, or a way to say "BSZ's rule with JR's move count."

11. The page told me the gap of about $146 billion is mostly explained by two parts: the income tax lost when billionaires leave (about 56%), and the court challenge (about 27%). It also said that the first part is one amount, because its three terms depend on the order you change them. So the page told me where to focus and that the split between parts is not fixed.

12. I am not sure. I read page 2 before the questions, and the 56% figure made the income tax part look like the one that mattered most, which may be why I went with JR's 45% on Q3 and its 50% on Q9. But I did not record that as a reason at the time, and my Q4 answer about Rauh's migration work is what I actually gave. The BSZ and JR labels did not change any answer that I can point to.

## Follow-up

1. The JR version says the loss is a flat 45% of all billionaires' income tax, whoever leaves, and that the 45% is set above the wealth that has left, to cover moves nobody reported. The BSZ version says only the income tax of those who leave is lost, in proportion to the wealth that leaves. I would pick BSZ now. The proportional wording says what the number means, while the JR version's "set above" reads like a plug to me. My original Q3 answer was JR because of Rauh's migration work, and this new JR wording makes me less sure that the 45% comes from the migration evidence at all.

2. On Q9, the BSZ addition ("drafted to stay clear of constitutional challenges") is a reason for the 100%, but it reads as advocacy, and I would discount it. The JR addition ("the more likely than not threshold an accounting rule uses") sounds like a recognised legal and accounting standard, and as a tax lawyer I read that as the better-lit wording. On Q11, the BSZ addition states a principle about legal residence ("a press report of a move is not a record of a change of legal residence"), and that is fuller than the original, so BSZ is now at least as well described as JR. JR's Q11 text is still a list of seven names with no reason for counting them, which makes it the thinner of the two.

3. Yes. The JR version gives the range inline, from $3.3 billion to $5.8 billion, and states the two assumptions that set each end, so I can see where the middle comes from. The BSZ version gives one step, "halved," with a reason that depends on research I would have to look up. A reader who does not know that research will take "halved" as an arbitrary adjustment. So JR is the fuller description and the better-lit one, and the phrase "the middle of a range" makes JR look cautious.
