# Interview transcript: respondent 17 (share, control)

## Round 1 (questions 1 to 7)

1. I think the survey was asking which numbers about Proposition 40 people find believable, and whether the tax would bring in the money it claims. It said it wasn't about being for or against the tax. I'm not sure whose side the numbers were meant to favor, and I didn't look closely.

2. The link said two analyses disagree and that the author was collecting expert views, so I expected to be asked about my expertise and then something about the tax. It did ask whether I was an expert first, but then it went straight into pairs of values, one choice per input. It was more detailed than I expected, but the form was the same as I had imagined: pick one of two.

3. It said about 5 minutes and I finished in about 3, so it felt quick. The longest part was Q7, the billionaires' income tax, because the dollar figures and the reasons for each took a second read to follow.

4. At Q13 I skipped the five smaller inputs because the survey said they had only a small effect, and I didn't see the point in going through them. I also left every "Why?" box blank because they were optional and I was moving fast. I skimmed the reasons under the first questions and mostly went by the first sentence of each option.

5. I'd make the options shorter, especially the long ones in Q7 and Q11, which were hard to take in quickly. I'd also say near the top what the "Why?" boxes are for, so I'd know whether it matters to fill them in. I'd probably drop the expert question at the start, since it doesn't change anything I was asked after it.

6. I read the "A." and "B." labels as plain option letters, like on a multiple-choice test. I didn't have an idea of who proposed either value. I had read some news about the tax before the survey, and the link named two analyses, but I didn't connect either letter to either analysis. I'm not sure whether that means the labels were unclear or that I just didn't look for who wrote them.

7. "About equally credible" meant I couldn't tell the two apart, and "Unsure" meant I didn't feel I knew enough to judge. I chose Unsure on Q7, the billionaires' yearly California income tax, because the three-number range and the halved figure were more than I could weigh quickly, and I didn't know which data was right. On the other questions I picked A or B when one sounded more plausible to me, and I didn't use "About equally credible" anywhere that I remember.

## Round 2 (questions 8 to 12)

8. Yes, in a few places one side had more words. In Q7, option A gives three numbers and explains where the top and bottom come from, while B is one short sentence about halving. In Q9, A is just "100%: the tax is collected in full, as written," but B says "a guesstimated likelihood," and that word made B sound like a guess to me. In Q11, A names seven billionaires, which reads as concrete, while B says counting them is an "aggressive scenario," which read as the survey leaning against it.

9. Q3 and Q9 were answers to the same question, so I could weigh one against the other. In Q3, A counts all billionaires' tax and B counts only the leavers' tax, so they answer the same thing, how much tax is lost. In Q9, both give a chance the tax survives, so I could compare them directly. Q11 is a bit different, because A is tied to seven named people while B is a general allowance with no names. I'm not sure whether that makes them two answers to the same question or two different ways of counting.

10. B in Q3 fits that person's view, because it is the only option that counts just the income tax of those who leave. The 29% in Q11 is a share of wealth, and the person would apply it to the leavers, so B is the one that goes with leavers only. A would count the tax of everyone, which doesn't match "only those who leave." I didn't think about this link when I answered, so this is my guess from reading the options.

## Follow-up

1. Option A says a flat 45% of the billionaires' income tax is lost whatever share leaves, plus extra for moves nobody reports. That reads as a loss bigger than the people who actually leave. Option B says only the leavers' income tax is lost, scaled to how much wealth leaves, so the loss follows the share that moves. I'd pick B now. On the original Q3 I picked A, and I think I picked it because "whoever leaves" sounded like it covered everyone, which I now see was the wrong reading.

2. In Q9, option A says the Act was drafted to stay clear of challenges, which reads like the proposition's defense. Option B cites a "more likely than not" rule from accounting, which sounds like a neutral, official standard. Option B looks more careful to me, though it still says 50% is a coin flip. In Q11, option A names seven billionaires, which feels concrete. Option B says a press report is not a record of a change of legal residence, which sounds like the more careful, legal standard, so it looks better to me. Neither value is described as clearly worse, but in both questions the B wording looked more official to me.

3. Option A gives a range from California tax data and says where the top and bottom come from, which sounds grounded in records. Option B says the number is "halved" because of a research finding, and "halved" sounds like an arbitrary cut to me. So A reads as better supported to me, and that's the value I would pick if I had to choose. I didn't weigh the numbers closely, so this is only the wording.
