# Interview guide (Opus interviewer, max effort), 2026-10-17

Put to ten simulated respondents in two messages: questions 1 to 7, then 8 to 12 (11 and 12 to treated respondents only). The interviewer's notes: question 5 comes before the probes so its answer is unprompted; 8 probes the reviewer's F4/F5, 10 probes F1, 9 touches both.

Please answer from what you saw and did when you took the survey. You can look back at the survey text. Short answers are fine, and so are "I didn't notice" and "I don't know".

1. In your own words, what was this survey for?
2. Think back to what you read just before the survey opened (the panel, the explorer page, or the message with the link). What did it lead you to expect the survey would ask you, and how did the survey compare?
3. How long did the survey feel to you, and which part, if any, took you longest?
4. Which parts of the survey, if any, did you skip or only skim, and why?
5. What, if anything, would you change about the survey, and why?
6. In Q3 to Q18, each of the two values had a short label in front of it, after the choice number. What did that label mean to you? If you had an idea of who proposed a value, where did that idea come from?
7. What did "About equally credible" mean to you, and what did "Unsure" mean? If you chose either one, in which question, and why?
8. Apart from the numbers themselves, were the two values in each question described in an even-handed way? If one was described more fully, or in a better light, than the other, say which question and which words.
9. Look again at Q3 (the share of billionaires' income tax lost when people leave), Q9 (the chance the tax survives a court challenge) and Q11 (billionaires who moved out before the 1 January 2026 residency date). In each, were the two values two answers to the same question, so that you could weigh one against the other? Where they were not, what was different about them?
10. Suppose someone finds the "about 29% of the wealth" value in Q11 credible, and also believes that only the income tax of those who leave is lost. Which choice in Q3, if any, fits that person's view, and why?
11. (Treated respondents only.) Your form had a page called "How the two estimates differ". In your own words, what did that page tell you?
12. (Treated respondents only.) Did that page, or the labels BSZ and JR on the values, change any of your answers? If so, which question, and how?

## The interviewer's priors on the Fable review (before the interviews)

- Most important is F1. The two values in Q3 differ in both the rule and the count of leavers. So someone who holds the BSZ rule with the JR count has no answer, and Q3 leans on Q11.
- Next come F4 and F5. A reason is given on one side only, a choice contains a rebuttal of the other, and "guesstimated" appears in no JR text in the repo. Then F3, F9 and F22: the entry texts promise a different task, or promise that no study is named.
- Least convincing is F1 as "duplicate" and "blocking". JR's 45% is a flat rule, so Q3 is tangled up with Q11 but is not a copy, and H1 stays valid. F5's own replacement text keeps the rebuttal. F7's claim that polarization is the "most plausible" effect is a guess, though S4 and S5 cost little to add.
- The review misses two things. Control respondents who came from the explorer have already seen which study holds each value, because the dials name them. And A is the JR value in four required questions but the BSZ value in Q9, which can mislead a control respondent who assumes A is always the same source.
