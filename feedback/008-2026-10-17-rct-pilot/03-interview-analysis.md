# What the pilot and the interviews show (Opus interviewer, max effort), 2026-10-17

Saved by the orchestrator from the interviewer's round-2 reply. Tabulations were made from `pilot/responses.json` and the ten transcripts in `interviews/`. The respondents are simulated, so this is evidence on wording, not on how experts would answer.

## Established

- F4 and F5 hold. All 10 interviewees flagged "guesstimated" in Q9 and the "aggressive scenario" sentence in Q11. They read that sentence as hurting both sides. 10 of the 18 reasons given with Unsure on Q9 cite the "guess" label. Several respondents repeat Q11 B's "press reports do not establish residence" as their own reason.
- Q3 has no stable meaning. While answering, 5 of 10 read the 5% as a count of leavers. In the interviews, 7 of 10 called it a share of the leavers' own tax, a different base from A. Six respondents read the 45% as counting the tax of people who stay. In the made-up case (question 10 of the guide), 9 of 10 matched B on its words. Only #85 asked for "BSZ's rule with JR's count".
- The review missed Q7. It drew 30 JR picks against 3 BSZ, and 6 interviewees named the length gap: three sentences of "tax data" against one line saying "halved". The explorer's tips rate the two values the other way round: BSZ's as research, JR's as guesswork.
- F3, F9 and F22 hold. About 20 of the 33 landing respondents read the panel as a quiz with right answers. 10 of the 18 treated share-link respondents noticed that "names neither analysis" was false for their form.
- The treatment page's main message gets across: all 5 treated interviewees restated the 56% and 27%. The note on the order of the terms does not (F8).
- "About equally credible" is 2% of required answers, against 45% for Unsure. On Q5 and Q9, Unsure often means "neither value is right".
- Careful readers took a median of 14 minutes, against the 5 the survey promises.

## Contradicted

- F1's "duplicates Q11". Q3 and Q11 agree in 79% of the respondents who took a side on both. The other pairs that leave out Q7 agree in 69% to 78%, so this is general lean, not one judgment counted twice. The Q3 rewrite still stands, but because Q3 is ambiguous.
- F7's polarization. The gap between pro-tax and anti-tax personas is 0.68 in control and 0.71 in treatment. The mean |Lean| is 0.34 against 0.33. The content already reveals the sides without labels, and explorer-path controls also read them off the dials. The pull toward neutral shows up as Unsure, +0.11 (SE 0.06), which the plan's S1 already measures.
- F13's primacy. In control, the value listed second won Q3 (21 to 9) and Q9 (18 to 4).

## Open

- H1 is +0.04 (SE 0.09), but question effects offset each other. Q3 moves toward JR (-0.34, SE 0.14) and Q7 away from it (+0.22, SE 0.10).
- Whether real experts read the items the way these agents do.
- Whether the rewrites read as even. They are untested (a follow-up round puts three candidate rewordings to the same ten respondents).
- F2, F6, F10 and F21, which no pilot can settle.
