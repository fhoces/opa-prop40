Open Policy Analysis for:

California Proposition 40, the BSZ side Work in progress

Proposition 40, the Billionaire Tax Act, would tax California's billionaires once, at 5% of their net worth, paid over five years. Its supporters score it at about $100 billion. This site reproduces that score from its two sources, the Boll, Saez and Zucman (BSZ) working paper (NBER WP 35218, August 2026) and the Galle, Gamage, Saez and Shanske (GGSS) expert report (July 2026), and marks which of its inputs are data and which are guesses. This site follows the Open Policy Analysis (OPA) framework, so what the analysis concludes, how it gets there, and what to run yourself stay in separate, checkable places.

What the model says

The BSZ side's preferred estimate is the benchmark row of Table 5 in Boll, Saez and Zucman (2026): 5% of the $2,307 billion that Forbes lists for 250 California billionaires on 1 July 2026, less 10% for avoidance and evasion, is $103.8 billion, collected once. The GGSS expert report makes the same computation and rounds it to $100 billion (p.4). The other rows are the authors' own sensitivity checks.

The yearly income tax loss has no end date in Table 5. The authors argue it should last about as long as the five payment years; the RJKDC side (Rauh, Jaros, Kearney, Doran and Cosso, who oppose the measure) counts it forever, which BSZ call the main driver of the gap between the two sides (PDF p.22, fn.29). The revenue itself moves with only two guesses: the avoidance rate, and whether anyone left California before 1 January 2026. See every input, by origin.

Human verification uses three levels: no human review, lightly checked (checked, but the author cannot yet explain it clearly) and understood (the author can explain it to others). The automated test suites are mechanical checks, not an outside review. No other person has verified this work, and the original authors have not reviewed it.
