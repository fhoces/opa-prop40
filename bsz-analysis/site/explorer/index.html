How much would the one-time billionaire tax raise, net?

Boll, Saez and Zucman (BSZ, 2026) put the one-time tax at about $100 billion of wealth tax. This Open Policy Analysis (OPA) adds its income tax effects to get the main estimate. Most inputs are guesswork. Move them and the estimate updates.

Defined as the wealth tax, plus the extra income tax from assets sold to pay it, minus the income tax lost to billionaires who leave, counted over 5 years (present value at 3% a year). The wealth tax is counted at face value. more

Notes and data

Horizon. The income tax lost to leavers recurs every year they stay away. BSZ argue the losses should be temporary and give 5 years, the payment period, as an example (PDF p.27); Table 5 takes no stand on how long they last. The main estimate counts 5 years at 3% a year, the middle of the JR analysis's 1.5% to 4.5% range (BSZ give no rate). The JR analysis counts it forever: see the comparison overview.

Two departures from the workbook. (1) Its income tax loss is a literal 5% (Tab5!H6); the explorer reads it as avoidance times the share leaving, identical at the authors' values. (2) Table 5 deducts the phase-in (the Act's lower rate between $1B and $1.1B) in row 2 but not row 4 (Tab5!F9). The explorer copies this, so with both switches on the estimate is higher than a consistent treatment.

Data. grid.csv (every setting), inputs.csv (every input and its origin), grid.js (what this page reads). The pipeline writes all three; a test fails if grid.js differs from its output.
