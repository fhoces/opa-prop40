Open Policy Analysis: reconciliation of two rival analyses

California's Billionaire Tax (Proposition 40): why the two revenue estimates differ Work in progress

Supporters score the one-time 5% tax on the net worth of California's billionaires at about {{ggss_headline}}; the RJKDC report (Rauh, Jaros, Kearney, Doran and Cosso) finds {{rauh_npv}} once lost income tax is netted out. This page puts both on one common output and walks from one number to the other, one disputed input at a time. Each side is also a full, independent Open Policy Analysis of its own; this page adds a reconciliation layer on top, following the Open Policy Analysis framework.

A common output definition

The two headlines answer different questions. The BSZ side (Boll, Saez and Zucman, plus the GGSS expert report by Galle, Gamage, Saez and Shanske) reports how much the one-time tax collects; the RJKDC side reports a net present value that subtracts income tax lost for ever. Before any step of a bridge means anything, both are put on the same two outputs:

(a) gross revenue = one-time wealth tax collected (b) net fiscal effect = (a) + extra income tax from assets sold to pay - present value of income tax lost to departures, over a horizon of H years

The horizon H is itself disputed (row 2 of the project's list of 8 disputed inputs, comparison/DISPUTES.md): the BSZ side counts the losses for about the 5 payment years, the RJKDC side for ever. It is therefore a dial on the bridge below, not a fixed choice. Discounting uses the RJKDC report's own range for the discount rate, r uniform between {{r_min}} and {{r_max}} (Rauh p.20 eq.24); the BSZ side takes no position on r.

The bridge, one disputed input at a time

One scoring function, score_common() (comparison/R/model.R, with a Python twin), scores both sides. Every disputed input is an argument that takes either the BSZ value or Rauh's. With every input at the BSZ value it returns BSZ Table 5 row 1 exactly ({{bsz_row1_exact}}); with every input at Rauh's value it returns the expectation of Rauh's own Monte Carlo up to a {{rauh_resid_b}} model difference that is shown as its own step. The bridge runs in the direction of Figure 2 of Hoopes (2026), the existing comparison described below, from GGSS to Rauh; BSZ row 1 is the checkpoint. Because the inputs interact (a departure matters more when the base is larger), the size of a step depends on the order the inputs are switched. The default bars are Shapley values: each step's switch averaged over all {{n_orders}} possible orders, so the steps add up exactly to the total gap whatever the reading order. "One order" shows a single sequence instead, the one Hoopes reads in.

The existing comparison: Hoopes (2026)

Jeffrey Hoopes, "Galle v Rauh: Comparing Two Revenue Estimates for California Billionaire Taxation" (SSRN 6428578, 7 pages), is the one published side-by-side of the two estimates. His Figure 2 (p.6) is a waterfall from the "Galle estimate" to "Rauh net fiscal effect". It prints no values, so the numbers in its column below are read off the chart: bar edges measured in a 300 dpi raster against the y-axis ticks (tick fit within {{hoopes_fit}}; reading uncertainty about {{hoopes_unc}} per bar; script comparison/tools/digitize_hoopes_fig2.py). Beside them is our bridge read in the same order, starting at the same printed $100B.

Disclosures from the note itself: Hoopes states his priors, "To lay my cards on the table, I believe that the world is complicated, and that when possible, empirical analysis should be done in as detailed a way as possible" (p.2), and thanks "David Gamage for encouraging me to read both of these analyses carefully and compare the two" (p.1); Gamage is a GGSS co-author.

Neutral material and credited alternative dials

LAO (neutral context). The Legislative Analyst's ballot analysis gives no point estimate: the tax "probably would collect tens of billions of dollars", and responses "probably would reduce state revenues by less than $1 billion per year" (LAO p.2). It enters here only as context. Walczak / California Tax Foundation (22 Apr 2026) is same-side work on the RJKDC side, credited as an alternative dial on the annual loss, never merged into Rauh's headline: his {{walczak_range}} per year includes spillovers to sales and other taxes (p.1).
