What the Billionaire Tax Act is worth to the state, by the RJKDC side’s method

Rauh, Jaros, Kearney, Doran and Cosso (RJKDC; Rauh et al. below) estimate the Act's fiscal effect as its one-time wealth tax revenue minus the present value of income tax lost for ever when billionaires leave: NPV = WT − f·C / r, where NPV is the net present value, WT the wealth tax collected once, f the share of the income tax base that leaves, C the annual California income tax that base pays, and r the discount rate. They report the average over 100,000 random draws of those inputs. This page recomputes that average, and the share of draws below zero, for every combination of seven inputs. It is this project's open output: all 2,187 combinations are precomputed, so the outcomes update as you click.

How this grid differs from the papers. Each cell is the exact expectation of the authors' formula (a closed form, since the draws are independent uniforms) and an exact share of negative outcomes (numerical integration, accurate to about 0.01 points), not a new random draw. At the two preferred settings this gives Neither gap is an error: the papers print one seeded run of 100,000 draws. Monte Carlo error on the mean is about ±0.5 ($B), four standard errors (one standard error is 38.6/√100,000, about 0.12). The labels after each input name (data, research, guesswork) follow the report's every input, by origin table. The litigation dial and the growth dial are readings the papers' text states but their code does not implement; see mismatches 2, 3 and 5.
